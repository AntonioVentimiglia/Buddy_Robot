**Tags:** calc, decision, process
- **Declutter:** cut the repo from 227 to 106 tracked files — deleted ~19
  template-only research folders, 7 stub ROS packages, duplicate requirements
  files. Removed the `(_IP)` filename convention entirely (files are always in

- **Motor sizing (calc):** built the per-wheel torque envelope for the 20 kg
  skid-steer base (r = 0.06 m, 4 driven wheels). Key finding: **pivot-in-place
  tire scrub on carpet sets the stall requirement** — 2.9–3.8 N·m/wheel at
  μ = 0.6–0.8, well above the worst straight-line case (1.47 N·m at 5° ramp +

  derived it from the resisting yaw moment about the pivot center.

  $54.99 ea) — the only shortlisted motor clearing the pivot band. Pololu 37D
  (2.65 N·m) and DDSM115 hub servos (2.0 N·m) fall inside/below it. Recorded in
  `docs/decisions/ADR-0003-drive-motor-selection.md` (Proposed; purchase pending).

  convention in `docs/portfolio/README.md`). Milestone report M01 written.
- **Wheel selection (decision + calc):** caught that the 0.06 m wheel radius used
  in sizing was an unsourced URDF placeholder. goBILDA traction wheels max out at
  96 mm; selected **4× Hogback 96 mm** (crowned tread purpose-built to reduce
  skid-steer scrub — directly attacks our dominant load case) + 8 mm REX hubs,

## 2026-07-16 — Motor datasheet arrives, catches a 4× odometry bug
**Tags:** failure, calc, build
- **The bug:** the motor spec sheet states *"Encoder Shaft: 28 Pulses per
  Rotation (7 Cycles)"* — 28/7 = 4, so goBILDA's "pulses" are **already
  4x-decoded quadrature counts**. My code, built from the vendor web page's
  "751.8 PPR", multiplied by 4 again → 3007 counts/rev instead of **753.2**.
  Odometry would have reported ~¼ of true distance. On bench day this would
  have looked like a baffling scale error and cost hours; the datasheet cost
  ten minutes.
- **Root cause is naming, not arithmetic:** "PPR" is ambiguous (pulses? cycles?
  edges?) and I resolved it the conventional way instead of checking. Fix
  renames the parameter to `encoder_counts_per_rev_output` so the unit is
  unambiguous at every call site, with the datasheet quote in the comment.
  Corrected in design_params, the generated firmware header, `base_core.py`,
  both test suites, and the mock MCU's wheel model; all 6 suites still green.
- **Caught a vendor error too:** the sheet's companion "(134.4 Cycles)" is
  inconsistent — 753.2/134.4 = 5.6, not 4, and 134.4 = 7 × **19.2**, the *other*
  gear ratio's number. Our value (28 × 26.9 = 753.2) is self-consistent. Flagged
  in the yaml comment; empirical check added to bench day (one wheel revolution
  by hand, read the count).
- **Confirmed by the same sheet:** no-load current **0.25 A** — exactly the
  estimate the power budget assumed and flagged for measurement (ADR-0005). One
  assumption closed, no change needed.
- **Harness gap found while purchasing:** connectors/cabling for the motors were
  never in the shopping list. Wrote `docs/system_model/wiring_harness.md`
  documenting goBILDA's MH-FC/FH-MC gender notation, the motor's two interfaces
  (3.5 mm bullets FH-MC for power; 4-pos JST XH FH-MC for the encoder), and —
  most importantly — that **motor power goes to the driver, never to the
  Nucleo**. Correct part is the *breakout* encoder cable (4 individual Dupont
  leads), because our encoder pins are deliberately non-adjacent across
  TIM2/3/4/8. +$56 to the cart (now ~$646, or ~$741 with Phase A sensors).

## 2026-07-31 — Drivetrain ordered; fabrication method chosen; the arms get real
**Tags:** purchase, decision, calc
- **Drivetrain ordered** (goBILDA): 4× 5203 motors, 4× Hogback wheels, 4× Sonic
  hubs, encoder breakout cables, motor power cables, M4 hardware — ~$359 with
  shipping, recorded in `Buddy_BOM.xlsx` as *Order Pending*. The 4× Pololu
  VNH5019 carriers and 2× NUCLEO-G474RE already arrived. ADR-0003/0004 are now
  decisions with money behind them and should flip Proposed → Accepted.
  *(First CAD assemblies — motor, wheel, Sonic hub, drivetrain — were committed
  the same session; that work still needs its own narrative entry from the
  author, including why the sub-assembly split was chosen.)*
- **Fabrication method decided (ADR-0008): in-house FDM printing**, and the
  printer is **QIDI Plus 4** (~$800). The interesting part is which requirement
  turned out to be binding. The instinct is to score printers on build volume
  and price; the actual driver was **chamber temperature**. Motor mounts bolt
  directly to gearboxes running at the 8 A design limit, and PLA's glass
  transition is ~60 °C — a mount that creeps there doesn't break visibly, it
  relaxes, and the symptom that reaches the operator is **drifting odometry**,
  which gets misdiagnosed as a firmware bug for weeks. That pushes the material
  to ASA/PA-CF, which at a ~280 mm part length needs an *actively* heated
  chamber, not a passive enclosure. That single requirement eliminated the
  popular defaults: the Bambu P1S (best measured accuracy in the set at
  ±0.05 mm) and the Elegoo Centauri Carbon are both passively enclosed, and the
  Prusa CORE One — the most open and best-supported machine considered — has the
  *smallest* build area of the five at the highest price.
- **Reversal worth recording:** the Centauri Carbon ($299) was the right
  recommendation an hour earlier, under a hard budget ceiling. When the owner
  scoped the printer as a capital tool for future projects, the ceiling lifted
  and the ranking inverted. Lesson: a recommendation is only as durable as the
  constraint that produced it — worth naming the constraint out loud so the
  reversal is legible instead of looking like inconsistency.
- **Arms decided (ADR-0009): printed structure + hobby servos**, forced by cost —
  Dynamixel XM430-class would be ~$3,100 for twelve joints against a $2000
  programme budget. **This decision reached back into the power architecture.**
  ADR-0005's whole argument was that stall current is a *designed ceiling*
  because the VNH5019s have an adjustable current limit. Hobby servos have no
  such limit — commanded into a stall they draw stall current until something
  else stops them. Bounded it: manipulation average ~39 W (the 45 W placeholder
  survives), realistic 8-joint peak ~11 A at the bus (10 A placeholder marginal),
  all-12-stalled ~29 A — which would have pushed the designed peak from 47 A to
  ~66 A, past both the ≥50 A BMS and the 60 A fuse.
- **The protection chain already contained it, by accident of good design.** The
  15 A arm branch fuse from the 2026-07-14 amendment caps the branch at ~166 W,
  sitting deliberately between the realistic peak (holds) and the all-stalled
  case (blows). So ADR-0005 survives unmodified and **the imminent battery
  purchase is not blocked** — checked, not assumed. What changed is the *role* of
  that fuse: specified as a backstop for actuators that limited their own
  current, it is now the primary limiter. Safe, but not graceful — the drive side
  degrades by quietly limiting torque, the arm side by blowing a fuse and
  demanding a human. Flagged for a current-limited servo rail at arm build time.
- **Budget reframed** (`design_conflicts.md` #4, revised): the $300–600 band is
  superseded by a $2000 programme budget split into pots — $1,173.85 spent,
  $826.15 left, v0.1 needs ~$355 of it, future-phase (OAK-D + arm servos)
  ~$380–560. v0.1 closes comfortably; the roadmap closes only at the low end of
  the servo estimate. That makes the ADR-0009 open item — evaluate Feetech
  STS/SCS bus servos, which would buy back both position feedback and current
  limiting at near hobby-servo cost — a budget decision as much as a technical one.
- Research doc: `docs/research/hardware/fabrication/printer_selection.md`
  (six requirements derived from the robot, five candidates scored, known
  limitations of the selected machine listed rather than discovered later —
  including that its effective *heated* build height is ~268 mm, not 280).

## 2026-07-12 — Power budget: the stall-current fear, resolved by design
**Tags:** calc, decision
- **The fear:** 4 × 9.2 A motor stall + Jetson + sensors looked like an
  impossible/unaffordable battery worst case. **The resolution:** stall current
  is a fault condition, not an operating point — the motor drivers' adjustable
  current limit turns the worst case into a *chosen* number. Designed ceiling:
  8 A/motor → 37 A bus peak including system loads, vs 42 A unlimited fault.
- **The validator earned its keep on first run:** the initial 7 A limit choice
  only allowed 2.81 N·m — below the 3.05 N·m worst-case carpet pivot. Raised to
  8 A (allows 3.23 N·m) and scaled the protection chain to match (BMS ≥ 40 A,
  fuse 50 A). `tools/build.py` now enforces these inequalities on every build
  (`buddy_calcs/power.py::validate`).
- **Architecture (ADR-0005):** 3S Li-ion + BMS (chosen over RC LiPo for the
  hardware protection layer near people/pets); motors direct on the bus; Jetson
  behind a regulated 12 V buck (dev kit input 9–20 V verified — sag can't brown
  out compute); protection chain driver-limit → BMS → 50 A slow-blow fuse → E-stop.
- **Capacity:** unchosen camera/LiDAR carry ≤ 5 W *reserved allocations* that
  become hard selection constraints. Allocation model (everything at full
  reserve): 66 W avg → 92 Wh → **3S ≥ 8 Ah, ~$60–100** guarantees the 60-min
  requirement; expected usage (46 W) gives ~85 min. Peak is a mild ≈ 4.5C —
  the peaks never drove the sizing; runtime did.
- New parametric analysis: `design_params.yaml` power section +
  `buddy_calcs/power.py` + `docs/analysis/power_budget_and_battery.py` notebook +
  `assets/figures/power_budget.svg`. Superseded and deleted the all-TBD
  `docs/hardware/power_budget.yaml` and `tools/calculators/battery_sizing.py`.
- **Carried forward:** motor driver selection now has a HARD requirement —
  adjustable ~8 A per-channel current limit (in-driver or current-sense + MCU).
- **Motor driver shortlist (decision pending purchase):** compared RoboClaw
  2x15A (premium, $260), TB67H420FTG (hardware current chop but 3.4 A continuous
  — only 10% over the 3.1 A sustained case, thermally thin), BTS7960 ($35,
  crude), and **4× Pololu VNH5019 carriers (~$100, recommended)** — 12 A/30 A
  per channel, 140 mV/A current-sense into the G474's ADCs for the 8 A firmware
  limit, hardware overcurrent backstop. The firmware limit sits on the safety
  path → mandatory bench verification before rolling. Bonus: the dual-VNH5019
  Arduino shield stacks directly on the NUCLEO-G474RE for the first bench loop.
  Doc: `docs/research/hardware/electronics/motor_drivers/driver_selection.md`.
- **Purchasing checklist created:** `docs/financials/SHOPPING_LIST_v0_1.md`
  (temporary) — full v0.1 cart ≈ **$540** of the $300–600 budget: drivetrain
  $292, electronics + power $249. Conflict #4 quantified, trim options listed.
- **Portfolio website generator built** (`tools/site/build_site.py`, runs inside
